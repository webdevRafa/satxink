## Copy and positioning recommendations

**Lead with the product category and the workflow.** The existing headline has a clear benefit, but “flash” alone can attract tattoo clients instead of software buyers. Keep “tattoo shop software” prominent in titles, introductions and feature-page headings. Position the product as a shop-branded system whose differentiators are artist invitations, flash publishing, approval-based requests, deposits and owner tools. Do not target “tattoo shops near me” on this software site.

Suggested expanded introduction for the next copy pass:

> Invite your artists, publish individual flash or full sheets, and turn client requests into approved appointments. Artists connect their own Stripe accounts for payments. Owners manage shop hours, events and team details from one dashboard—all under your shop’s brand.

**Show ownership of each step.** Use a real annotated walkthrough: owner invites artist → artist uploads sheet or design → client requests → artist offers times and deposit → client accepts and pays → artist tracks the booking. Explain payouts separately from client deposit collection. Avoid promises of instant payouts or unrestricted self-booking.

**Make setup concrete.** The full website versus companion portal distinction is good. Add an implementation checklist, what the owner supplies, who configures Stripe, where the portal lives, how invitations work and what happens after the walkthrough. Publish installation timelines only when you can support them.

**Make price scope explicit.** Preserve the existing $500 setup and $100/month support pricing. The copy already discloses database and email costs. Before adding a dedicated pricing page, confirm how Stripe processing fees, client service fees, custom work, cancellations and usage costs are explained. Do not invent a fee schedule from assumptions.

**Add evidence and identity.** Use actual owner-dashboard and artist-workspace screenshots with explanatory captions. Add a founder/about page identifying who provides setup and support. Add named customer stories only after receiving permission and having actual outcomes. Avoid unverified “first,” “only,” “best,” unlimited-capacity or guaranteed-booking claims.

**Correct stale supporting copy.** Privacy and website-information pages still discuss an optional 3D scene and imply that visuals are not software screenshots, while the homepage now features product recordings and a screenshot. Review those factual descriptions before launch. If analytics or a contact form is introduced later, update the corresponding data-handling description too.

**Reduce inquiry friction.** A short demo-request form could improve conversion for visitors without an email app. This is a conversion recommendation, not an indexing requirement. The current mailto workflow remains functional and was not changed.

Keep the homepage as the overview. Feature pages should explain different jobs and link to one another naturally. Each needs a unique title, H1, description, canonical, substantial original content, relevant screenshots and a walkthrough CTA. One sitemap can hold up to 50,000 URLs subject to its size limit. The current three pages and this proposed expansion need only one sitemap. ## Exact Search Console and launch steps

1. **Submit the existing sitemap now.** In the verified `satxink.com` property shown in your screenshot, open Indexing → Sitemaps, paste **https://www.satxink.com/sitemap.xml** into “Add a new sitemap,” and submit. Use the full www URL even though the domain property is named satxink.com. This URL is already live; deployment is not required just to submit it.
2. **Publish the reviewed local changes.** Use the existing production workflow. The build command must be `npm run build` and the published directory must be `dist`. Do not publish `dist-ssr` or the unbuilt source HTML. This audit did not publish anything.
3. **Check production delivery.** Open homepage, privacy, terms, robots and sitemap. Check that an invented URL returns 404, not 200. Check that apex/HTTP variants lead to HTTPS www and the about/contact/FAQ aliases redirect properly. Inspect page source to confirm the actual copy and each page's own canonical are present.
4. **Inspect the homepage in Search Console.** Paste `https://www.satxink.com/` into the inspection bar. Review the indexed result and any exclusion reason, then choose **Test live URL**. Inspect the tested page's HTML/screenshot. Confirm crawling is permitted, the page loads, content is visible and the declared canonical is the www homepage. If the live result is indexable, choose **Request indexing**. Repeat for the important new feature pages when published.
5. **Read Indexing → Pages.** Distinguish discovered-not-indexed, crawled-not-indexed, duplicate/canonical, blocked, noindex and soft-404 reasons. Use the affected URL examples to decide the fix. A successful sitemap submission does not resolve every exclusion automatically. Do not repeatedly resubmit the same URL to try to accelerate crawling. [Google recrawl guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/ask-google-to-recrawl)
6. **Exclude the separate demo.** Configure `X-Robots-Tag: noindex, follow` on the demo's HTML responses, or a reliable initial-HTML noindex meta. Leave crawling allowed so Google can see it. Do the same for public preview copies as appropriate. Apply this to the demo/preview deployment only. Never put that header on the production marketing site. [Google noindex guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/block-indexing)
7. **Validate structured data and mobile performance.** Test the deployed homepage with Google's Rich Results Test and PageSpeed Insights. Check mobile layout, LCP, INP and CLS using available field data; a new site may lack field data. The two demo recordings total roughly 8 MB on disk, although the existing component limits playback to visible videos. Measure actual mobile loading before changing media or fonts. Do not treat a local build as proof of passing Core Web Vitals.
8. **Launch and measure.** Publish the first feature pages, request indexing, and announce the launch through your own relevant channels. Ask real partner shops to link to your product where useful; avoid purchased bulk links. Review Search Console weekly for indexed canonical pages, non-brand impressions, relevant owner-intent queries and clicks. Track qualified walkthrough inquiries separately from search traffic. If adding analytics, define meaningful events and update the site's disclosures.

Crawling and indexing can take days or weeks, and neither submission nor correct markup guarantees inclusion or rankings. Do not make September 20 search visibility a launch dependency. The immediate controllable goals are correct delivery, a submitted sitemap, clear Texas messaging, useful commercial pages and a working inquiry path. [Google recrawl guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/ask-google-to-recrawl)
